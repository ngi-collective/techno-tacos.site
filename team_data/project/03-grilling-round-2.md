# Grilling round 2

The team accepted every recommendation, session of 2026-11-03.

1. Light panel: stays on the box's light-facing side all the time, with no key.
2. Off key: removes both the box and the tunnel, leaving a plain beach with a nest.
3. Confusing lights: a row of bright beach houses and streetlights behind the dunes. The light panel faces them.
4. Predators: crabs on the sand, birds overhead, and a raccoon near the nest.
5. Predator behavior: predators grab hatchlings and carry them away, with nothing gory.
   - No tunnel: they catch many.
   - With the tunnel: they prowl outside and can't get through the walls. One sometimes catches a hatchling at the mouth. A sign says "Tunnel mouth risk: we asked Turtle Beach Rehab."
6. Counter: shows hatchlings that reached the ocean, got lost, or were caught. Label: "Simulation, not real data."
7. Holding the tunnel down: sand adds weight and stakes keep wind from moving it.
8. Setup steps, done by two simple volunteer figures:
   1. Find the marked nest.
   2. Place the box over it.
   3. Clip on the light panel.
   4. Attach the tunnel through the hole.
   5. Stretch the tunnel to the waterline.
   6. Add sand inside and hammer in the stakes.
   7. Turn on the guiding lights.
9. Setup timing: plays once in daylight when the page starts, then leads into sunset and hatching. Only the hatching loops after that. A key replays setup.
10. Real-size placeholders, marked on a sign: hatchling 5 cm long, tunnel 30 cm wide, box 1 m wide.
11. People problems: holes, sandcastles, chairs, and trash appear in the no-tunnel scene, and hatchlings get stuck on them.
